Computer Vision Techniques on Astronomical Object

This project involved analyzing a rotating astronomical Earth model suspended in the UCL Marshgate building. The work was divided into four major tasks, progressing from image segmentation to 3D estimation and dynamic rotation analysis.

⸻

Task 1 — Robust Object Segmentation

Developed a robust pipeline to extract the Earth model from video frames under varying lighting and backgrounds.

•	Implemented and compared multiple segmentation approaches:

•	Color Thresholding (HSV filtering).

•	Ellipse Fitting on detected contours.

•	Minimum Enclosing Circle detection.

•	Evaluated performance using ROC curves.

•	Final Solution: A hybrid approach combining color thresholding + ellipse fitting, achieving the most stable and accurate segmentation across all datasets.

✅ Outcome: Reliable AO extraction across different viewpoints and lighting conditions.

⸻

Task 2 — Spatial & Motion Analysis

2a — Centroid Detection

Used circle/ellipse fitting on segmented frames to extract the geometric center of the Earth model per frame, supporting real-time or video processing.

2b — Oscillation Modeling

  • Tracked changes in the model’s center over time to model its swing as a 2D pendulum, fitting: $x(t)=A_x\sin(\omega_x t+\phi_x) + C_x,\quad y(t)=A_y\sin(\omega_y t+\phi_y)+C_y$
  • Revealed consistent oscillation frequency and larger movement in the x-axis compared to the y-axis.

2c — Height Estimation via Stereo Triangulation

Used two horizontally spaced cameras to triangulate 3D points on the AO.

•	Feature detection → Matching → Triangulation

•	Achieved 20.43 m estimated height, close to 21.4 m ground-truth (~4.5% error)

✅ Outcome: Accurate 2D motion modeling and 3D height reconstruction.

⸻

Task 3 — Estimating Rotation Period

  • Designed a robust algorithm to determine the complete rotation cycle of the model from video:

    1. Detect keypoints using ORB / SIFT

    2. Filter keypoints to those on the AO surface

    3. Track matches frame-to-frame

    4. Identify the frame that best matches the first frame → indicates a full rotation

  • Added multithreading to achieve near real-time processing.

  • Algorithm matched rotation period results accurately across multiple recordings and viewpoints.

✅ Outcome: Consistent rotation estimates such as 8–9 minute cycles across sequences.

⸻

Task 4 — Diameter Measurement & Radius Consistency

Computed real-world diameter using camera intrinsics, image pixel radius, and geometric projection formulae.

•	Estimated diameter ≈ 5.44 m vs true 7 m → ~22% underestimation.

•	Verified radius consistency by comparing fitted ellipses across frames → changes < 1% in major/minor axes.

✅ Outcome: Method is reliable in principle; accuracy mainly limited by camera FOV + viewing angle.
